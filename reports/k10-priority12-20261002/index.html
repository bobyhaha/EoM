Can shared drafts and reliable closing improve team problem solving?

Both fixes together had the highest observed score. Shared drafting alone was the most expensive and delivered a modest gain. All team episodes produced answers; original EoM failed to finalize physics under both seeds.

What worked—and what did not

  • Both fixes: mean 0.498 versus baseline 0.418. Cost $1.723 versus $1.286, about 34% more. Physics and biology account for the improvement; chemistry remains weak.
  • Finalization only: mean 0.443, about 11% cheaper than baseline. All six unpaid closures occurred without a new bid or extra reward-path credit. However, the baseline also answered all six tasks, so this study does not establish a completion-rate benefit.
  • Shared drafting only: mean 0.453, about 51% more expensive than baseline. This is insufficient evidence to justify two full passes every round as the default.
  • Original EoM: very cheap ($0.041 across six episodes), but no final physics answer in either seed. Both traces ended when nobody activated after a planner action. The answer role was gated off at that point.

Departures are leave events, not necessarily successful switches to a new team. Invalid decisions are deduplicated by episode, round, agent and phase; one malformed response can generate multiple error events. A no-submission round means paid activity did not produce a published candidate, not that its discussion necessarily had no value.

  • Membership inertia: most teams formed early and stayed stable. Five departures occurred in seed-17 baseline episodes; none in the other configurations. Closure/physics seed 7 and baseline/chemistry seed 7 remained entirely solo.
  • Misunderstood historical rewards: agents repeatedly said staying would preserve past contribution credit. Recorded membership already preserves that credit after departure. This is evidence of stated reasoning, not proof of internal causality.
  • Waiting for complementary expertise: chemistry agents repeatedly declined invitations because nobody had verified source-specific information. Fresh, similarly prompted agents had little evidence of differing capabilities.
  • Consensus without correction: chemistry discussions reinforced a cautious answer that rejected empirical DLS peak values and definite substitution outcomes. The benchmark expects those particulars. Repeated revisions mostly polished the same position; chemistry scores were only 0.12–0.20.
  • Paid rounds without publication: shared-draft and combined configurations each had nine no-submission rounds, versus zero in baseline and closure. Null candidates or lack of a publishable intermediate candidate can consume a paid round.

Verification

All ten workers finished normally, with three results each and no terminal worker errors. Receipts reconcile with status spending; every per-cell cap was respected; no unresolved reservations remain. Configuration checks confirm k=10, seeds 7/17, fixed bids and the intended two experimental switches. All recorded unpaid closures charged zero and added no path credit. Reward events reconcile issued wealth with member credits and give each recorded contributor the same per-round share. Experiment source fingerprints still match the launch manifest. No extra paid tests or retries of completed cells were launched.

Interpretation limits

This is three development tasks repeated under two local seeds, not six independent tasks or a held-out study. All populations start fresh; no cross-task training or evolution is evaluated. One model supplies the agents and judge; grading was not independently repeated in this pilot. Matching local seeds does not fix API sampling. Original agents have roles and frozen evaluation economics; team agents have different prompts, payments and substantially more compute. Thus these results do not isolate teaming itself or reproduce the paper’s trained-population comparison. Concurrency increased during the run at the user’s request; elapsed-time comparisons are therefore confounded. No significance claim is warranted.

Recommended next ablations

  1. Clarify explicitly that switching never forfeits historical credit, without changing the reward formula.
  2. Remove default-stay wording and let agents assess an invitation before committing to leave, preserving consent.
  3. Shorten pre-bid discussion and test one drafting pass with concise corrections; require a specific unresolved issue before another pass.
  4. Test readiness-to-publish separately from willingness-to-pay, while preserving abstention.
  5. Give every comparator equal source-retrieval access for knowledge-heavy tasks, without leaking the reference answer.
  6. Add a compute-matched comparison and independent grading before drawing a performance conclusion.

No experimental treatment was changed to implement these recommendations in this pilot.
